# Afro-Lust: how to sing on it

Intimate, relaxed, confident, never shouted. Close to the listener's ear.

## The feel in one line

Sing like you are talking to one person, close to their ear, with nothing to prove. Relaxed, confident, never pushed. If you feel yourself trying hard, you are too loud.

## Delivery: mic distance and breath

- Hold the phone about a hand's width (10–15 cm) from your mouth, a little below your chin and angled up, so breaths and the letters P and B do not hit the mic.
- Aim for a soft, slightly breathy tone on most lines: let a little air in before the note, but keep the pitch steady. Breathy is not weak; it is controlled.
- Sing at about 60% of your full volume. The beat is quiet and spacious, so a quiet voice will sit on top of it and still feel close.
- End lines by fading out, not by pushing. Let the last word fall away.

## Where to sit in your range

- Use the lower half of your comfortable range for verses: this is where the voice sounds warm and close. Think low, slow, almost spoken.
- Move up only a little in the chorus (two or three notes), and keep that lift soft. Save your highest notes for one or two moments, as a sigh, not a belt.
- If a note feels like effort, drop the whole line down. The key can be changed in Beat Lab (Key and Scale), so pick the key that makes your verses feel easy.
- Do not chase perfect notes. A small waver or slide into a note sounds human and intimate; sliding from slightly below a note is the classic late-night sound.

## Ride the groove: stay behind the beat

- The beat leans back on purpose. Start your words a touch LATE, after the rim hit, not on top of it. Count the groove like a slow sway: one-and-two-and, and land your main words just behind the count.
- Stretch the vowels over the long bass notes and keep the consonants light. The bass is the part people feel; you ride on top of it.
- Let the heartbeat (the double kick-and-bass pulse at the start of each bar) be your clock. Breathe in on the gap after it.
- If you rush, the song turns into a normal slow jam. Slower and later is the sound.

## When to whisper, hum and open up

- Whisper: the intro, the first line of a verse, and any line you want the listener to lean in for. Keep it supported by air, not just quiet.
- Hum: in the gaps between lines and in the breakdown (the bridge, where only bass and percussion play). A hum on the root note of the key is a safe, warm choice.
- Open up (still soft): the last chorus, and one line near the end of a verse. Open means more tone and a little more volume, not shouting.
- A good shape for a whole song: whisper, speak-sing, soft sing, one open moment, then back to a whisper at the end.

## Ad-libs, harmonies and double-tracking

- Ad-libs: short, low and breathy, answering the main line in the gaps. One or two sounds ('mm', 'ah', 'ooh', a word from the line) are enough. Put them quietly and a little to the side.
- Harmony: sing the main line, then record a second take three notes above (or one octave up) very softly, only on the ends of lines or in the chorus. A harmony that is quieter than the lead feels like a hand on your shoulder.
- Double-track: record the same line twice, as close to the same phrasing as you can. Mix the second one lower. Do this on the chorus, not the whole song, so the chorus feels bigger and closer at the same time.
- Use a different feel on each take: one slightly breathy, one slightly more spoken. Small differences make it sound wide.

## Phrasing, pacing and silence

- Say less. Short lines with space after them sound more confident than lines packed with words.
- Leave a full beat or two of silence after the strongest line of each verse. Let the bass and the guitar lick answer you.
- Do not fill every bar. In the breakdown, sing nothing, or only hum, and let the percussion talk.
- Land the title or the key phrase right after a gap, slowly, and repeat it once with a different tone, softer or lower.
- Keep your breaths. A quiet breath before a line is part of the sound here, so do not edit every breath out.

## Recording on a phone (cleanest, warmest sound)

- Record in a small room full of soft things: a bedroom with a bed and clothes, or inside a wardrobe. Avoid bathrooms and bare rooms (echo).
- Hold the phone with the mic (usually the bottom edge) toward your mouth, and keep the distance the same on every take. Use a pop-screen or a thin sock over the mic if you have no pop filter.
- Wear wired or Bluetooth earbuds to hear the beat, so it does not leak into the mic.
- Put the phone on 'Do not disturb' so nothing rings mid-take, and turn off any voice enhancement or noise reduction that makes you sound thin.
- Do one test line, listen back, and adjust the distance. Record several full takes and keep the best bits. Warm and close beats loud: do not move away from the mic to get volume, use the volume of the beat instead.
- Do not add effects while recording. Keep the take dry. The reverb and space come from the mix.
